What we build

Useful software.
Real-world purpose.

Web applications, purposeful automation and connected services. We build around practical problems: information that is hard to navigate, work that repeats itself and processes that ask too much of the people using them.

Web applications 01Automation 02Connected services 03Where it fits
01 / Web applicationsW.

From a complicated task
to a clear next step.

A good web application makes the work easier to understand. It gives people the right information, asks useful questions and helps them reach an outcome with confidence.

We focus on the complete journey: how someone starts, what they need to provide, how a decision is explained and what happens afterwards. The interface and the rules behind it need to work together.

Where it can help

  • Guided financial and administrative tasks.
  • Calculations that depend on several inputs or conditions.
  • Reporting, applications and information gathering.
  • Tools that replace a difficult spreadsheet or fragmented process.

What needs care

  • Clear labels, useful explanations and sensible defaults.
  • Validation that helps people correct a mistake.
  • Readable results and an understandable next action.
  • Responsive layouts and different ways of navigating.

The aim: make the task understandable, even when the underlying rules are complicated.

02 / Intelligent automationA.

Let the repetitive work
take care of itself.

Automation is useful when it removes effort without removing the understanding or control people need.

A repeatable calculation, a rule-based workflow or a well-designed integration may be all a task needs. AI can help when interpreting or organising information adds value, with suitable checks and a clear understanding of its limitations.

Where it can help

  • Turning structured information into a useful output.
  • Checking inputs against agreed conditions.
  • Preparing drafts or summaries for someone to review.
  • Reducing repeated entry and routine handovers.

What needs care

  • The quality and suitability of the information used.
  • Knowing which decisions require human review.
  • Handling exceptions and uncertain results.
  • Making important actions visible and traceable.

The aim: less repetition, with a clear place for judgement.

03 / Connected servicesC.

A better journey
between the systems.

People should not have to understand every system behind a service just to get something done.

Connected software can move the right information between tools, reduce duplicated effort and make a process easier to follow. Good integration design also considers ownership, permissions, availability and what happens when a dependency is unavailable.

Where it can help

  • Moving information between existing tools.
  • Routing a request to the relevant service or team.
  • Bringing related information into one working view.
  • Keeping a person informed about the next step.

What needs care

  • Clear responsibilities for data and actions.
  • Appropriate access to the systems involved.
  • Useful responses when information is missing or delayed.
  • Managing changes to an external service over time.

The aim: a coherent experience across the whole process.

Where it makes a difference

Everyday complexity.
Thoughtfully resolved.

Business & finance

More confidence in
the important details.

Business tasks often combine numbers, deadlines, documents and decisions. A clearer product can help people understand what information matters and what to do with the result.

  • Financial tools: guided inputs, calculations and explanations.
  • Administration: organised information and fewer repeated steps.
  • Decision support: visible assumptions, relevant options and clear outputs.

The design challenge is to make a complicated process approachable while preserving the details that matter.

Public services

A clearer connection
between people and services.

A person reporting an issue should be able to explain it, provide the right information and understand what happens next. The service receiving it needs information it can use.

  • Reporting: structured descriptions and relevant context.
  • Guided journeys: useful questions in a sensible order.
  • Communication: understandable confirmation, routing and next steps.

The design challenge is to respect both sides of the interaction: the person asking and the service responding.

The choices underneath

The right technology
starts with the right problem.

Technology choices should serve the task, the people using the product and the people maintaining it. Adding a dependency or an automated step has consequences as well as benefits.

We consider the experience, the information involved, the cost of operating the product and the effort of changing it later. Accessibility, privacy and reliability belong in that conversation from the beginning.
